# Fatal Flags and Score Caps

Use fatal flags to avoid false-positive ideas that look good because they are easy to build or fun to imagine.

## Fatal Flags

| Flag | Meaning | Score cap |
|------|---------|-----------|
| No clear payer | Users complain, but nobody obvious pays | 60 |
| Payer/user mismatch | The annoyed person is not the buyer and cannot influence purchase | 60 |
| Crowded market, no wedge | Strong incumbents, high ratings, no specific underserved segment | 55 |
| Few competitors, no WTP | Absence of competitors may mean absence of market | 55 |
| App-quality-only pain | Complaints are bugs/support glitches, not unmet demand | 60 |
| Distribution dead end | Hard to reach users without ads/platform dependence | 60 |
| Regulated/liability-heavy | Healthcare, finance, legal, child safety, or compliance burden without domain edge | 55 |
| Integration bottleneck | Value requires hard integrations with closed/enterprise systems | 60 |
| AI-wrapper trap | AI is proposed but workflow pain, trust, and paid demand are unproven | 50 |
| Novelty-only | Sounds cool, but no repeated painful behavior exists | 45 |

## How to Apply

1. Score normally.
2. List all fatal flags.
3. Apply the lowest relevant cap to the 0-100 opportunity score.
4. Explain what would remove the cap.

Example:

```markdown
Fatal flag: Crowded market, no wedge. Score capped at 55.
To remove cap: find a recurring underserved segment with direct evidence and paid alternatives.
```

## Do Not Overuse

Fatal flags are not excuses to skip analysis. They are for preventing optimism after evidence has been checked.
